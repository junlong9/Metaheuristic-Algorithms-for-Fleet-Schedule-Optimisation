============================================================ SLIDE 4
SIMULATED ANNEALING
---
What simulated annealing is
---
1 · Search
---
Same priority-based selection neighbour search algorithm
---
2 · Accept
---
Better moves are always kept. Worse moves can still be kept early on, by chance.
---
3 · Cool down
---
Over time we accept fewer worse moves, so the search settles on a strong plan.
---
Why it helps
---
Think of the search like walking over hills. Today’s method only walks uphill. That is fast and safe, but you can get stuck on a steep part of the hill when a better peak is nearby.

Simulated annealing sometimes takes a downhill step early, so you can leave a trap and reach a better overall schedule. As the search “cools,” those downhill steps become rare, and we settle on the best plan we found.

============================================================ SLIDE 5
HOW IT FITS
---
How annealing fits into our design
---
KEEP · How we search today
---
• Focus on the worst availability days first
Rebuild the aircraft schedule after each move
Update availability without recalculating everything
Try larger timing shifts, then smaller ones
---
CHANGE · How we accept moves
---
Currently: only keep the improving moves
New method: annealing accept better moves always, worse moves sometimes, depending how early and late
Drop best improvement strategy so worse candidates can be tried
---
Verdict: Keep how we find candidate moves. Change how we decide which moves to accept.

============================================================ SLIDE 6
FAIR ACCEPTANCE
---
Fair testing
---
1 · Better plan?
---
If the new schedule improves worst-day availability, keep it. That always comes first.
---
2 · Worse worst-day?
---
If it lowers the minimum availability, reject it. Do not accept a “smoother” plan that makes the busiest day worse.
---
3 · Same floor?
---
If the worst day is unchanged, but the plan is bumpier or lower on average, allow it sometimes early, less often as the search cools.
---
What we report, and how we test
---
Because annealing is random, run the full search 3 times and report the typical result. Current priority selection NS method needs one run, do not compare the best of three with one baseline run.

============================================================ SLIDE 8
SENSITIVITY
---
Which settings actually change the result?
---
Hyperparameters to test
---
How far we move a check.
How long we let the search run (max iterations) after introducing SA.
Cooling criteria: how quickly we become stricter.
Chance of accepting a worse move 
These choices affect both schedule quality and runtime
---
What we will do
---
Change one setting at a time on the heavy forecast to properly measure it without interference.
Monitor the smoothness and runtime compared to baseline model.
Keep a list of settings that works and ignore the rest. Keep iterating to find the best hyperparameter.

============================================================ SLIDE 9
STARTING POINT
---
Separate start effect from algorithm gain
---
Why the start matters
---
The search only makes local moves from the original plan.
A well-staggered start can look like a better algorithm.
A bunched start can make a good method look weaker.
---
What we will do
---
Build a few controlled starts: bunched, as-loaded, and more spread out.
Run current method and annealing from each of those same starts.
Document how much the initial stagger impacts the optimisation.
---
Priority targeting does not cancel this.
---
It only chooses which events to try first. Days that are not the current worst days keep the original stagger, so a good start can still do some of the work.

============================================================ SLIDE 11
DECISION CRITERIA
---
What we will keep, and when we stop
---
Keep it if:
---
Worst-day availability must be as good as current algorithm.
Runtime does not increase too much.
New operational rules still produce legal, usable schedules.
The gain is from the method, not a luckier start or a luckier seed.
---
Drop it if:
---
Annealing does not beat the current method, and we  will keep the current method.
Worse moves start hurting the minimum availability,  we tighten the acceptance rule.
New constraints break feasibility.
