Main findings
In Semester 2, I extended the successful Iteration 1 priority-based selection approach with simulated annealing acceptance and a basin-hopping search, added prototype operational constraints, analysed search settings, and compared controlled starting schedules. The main recommendation is to retain priority-based selection with tuned neighbourhoods. The annealing experiments produced small smoothness gains but did not justify replacing the tuned baseline.
Worst-day availability 19 is impossible for the current forecast under the modelled maintenance rules. Six unavoidable D-check intervals overlap, limiting availability to 18. The priority method already reaches that bound.

1 Baseline and maintenance model
Iteration 1 established that prioritising checks on the worst availability days gives a practical deterministic search. The execution plan records an improvement from 15 to 18 aircraft and a runtime of approximately 15.4 seconds. The later local tuning record reproduces the published baseline at 18 aircraft, standard deviation 1.413971, and 14.6 seconds. I use those reproducible local scores for Semester 2 comparisons rather than combining rounded values from older slides. [S1, S2, S10]

The code scores schedules lexicographically: maximise worst-day availability first, minimise the standard deviation of daily availability second, and maximise mean availability third. Standard deviation uses the full daily series and the population convention in NumPy. A lower standard deviation means a smoother availability profile. A smoother schedule cannot outrank another schedule with better worst-day availability. [S2]

The sequence is A, B, A, C, A, B, A, D. A check can start within its legal start window and must not overlap another check on the same aircraft. When a check moves, the aircraft’s later checks are rebuilt using the interval reset rules. Larger checks reset the corresponding smaller-check clocks. The new start must lie inside the recalculated legal window, even when it moves later than its current start. A check cannot start after its due date, but it may finish after that date. [S2]
The baseline tries shifts of 28, 14, 7, and 3 days in either direction, in that order, with a 400-iteration cap. It searches the first priority level containing an improving neighbour and chooses the best improvement in that level. Availability is updated by removing the aircraft’s old maintenance intervals and adding the rebuilt ones. [S2]
Precision correction: older notes call the horizon 4480 days. The current code treats 4480 as an inclusive index and computes 4481 daily observations. The quoted means and standard deviations use those 4481 observations.

2 Integration of simulated annealing
The main integration is in savn_priority_sa.py. It reuses the baseline’s priority ordering, check rebuild, availability update, and scoring. The change is that feasible non-improving moves can enter the search. This matters because the original generate_neighbors function filters out every move that does not improve the score before an acceptance rule can see it. [S2, S3]

Classic simulated annealing
The classic SA function warm-starts from the published priority baseline by default, proposes a random feasible neighbour, applies the acceptance test, and multiplies temperature by cooling_rate after each iteration. The default temperature is 1.0 and the default cooling rate is 0.95. It stores a current schedule for exploration and a separate best-so-far schedule for reporting. This version contains the cooling mechanism described in the plan. [S3]
Acceptance rule
An improvement in the ranked score is accepted immediately. For a non-improving score, the loss is measured on the first worsened component: aircraft lost on the worst day, otherwise the increase in standard deviation, otherwise the decrease in average availability. The acceptance probability is the exponential of negative loss divided by temperature. At nonpositive temperature the code rejects non-improvements. At positive temperature an exact score tie has zero loss and is accepted. [S3]
The tightened option rejects a lower worst-day availability before the temperature test. The usual candidate permits a temporary reduction in worst-day availability, while retaining the best-so-far result. Every proposed move still has to satisfy maintenance rules and any active operational constraints. Temperature changes acceptance of legal schedules; it does not legalise a prohibited check movement. [S3, S4]
Difference from the execution plan
Slide 6 says that a lower worst day is always rejected. The later recorded decision allows that loss in the candidate and keeps the stricter rule as a fallback. Slide 4 describes cooling, which applies to classic SA. The measured basin-hopping candidate instead uses a fixed escape temperature throughout each run. Those distinctions should be stated in the upcoming presentation. [S1, S3, S8]

3 Basin hopping and earlier experiments
After classic SA failed to improve the published baseline, the search developed into basin-hopping with tabu polish. It first runs priority-based polish. Each escape cycle restarts from the protected best-so-far schedule, deliberately seeks a legal move with lower worst-day availability, then makes a plateau walk before polishing again. The exact pre-escape start vector is temporarily tabu during that polish so the search cannot immediately restore the same schedule. Only an improved polished result replaces best-so-far. [S3, S9]
The final decision settings use escape temperature 1.5, 15 escape cycles, 30 plateau moves, and up to 400 polish iterations. The escape loop permits one initial move plus up to 30 subsequent moves per cycle. There is no cooling of escape_temperature in basin_hopping_optimise. The settling stage is deterministic polish. Interpretation
The gain is real in the saved best-so-far scores, but it is small compared with the extra search effort. The recommendation is to retain the tuned priority baseline. The candidate’s timed-out results are best-so-far snapshots at termination, not evidence that all planned cycles completed or that the search converged. [S7]
The timeout checks occur between iterations and escape moves rather than inside neighbour evaluation. A long evaluation can therefore overrun the nominal 300-second limit. The recorded overshoots range up to 721.3 seconds in the settings sweep. The measured elapsed time and timeout flag, rather than a claim of an exact five-minute cap, should be reported. [S3, S6, S7]
All tightened runs match the tuned baseline. In the present basin-hopping implementation the first escape move must lower worst-day availability, while tightened acceptance forbids that move. Thus the tightened option cannot start its escape phase and effectively returns the warm-start polish. Its shorter recorded times do not show a new successful annealing search. [S3]

5 Why worst day availability 19 is impossible
This result follows from mandatory maintenance occupancy. It is independent of how many random seeds or parameter combinations are tried. The current workbook contains 24 aircraft. Guaranteeing availability 19 would require no more than five aircraft in maintenance on every day. [S2, S5, S15]
Unavoidable interval of a D check
A first D check lasts 126 days and may start from 70 days before its due date through to its due date. The earliest placement finishes 56 days after due; the latest placement begins on due. Every legal placement therefore covers the 56-day interval from the due date to 56 days after it, with the end date excluded. In the report notation this is [due, due + 56).
The first D due date is fixed by the opening due date plus 7 times 365 days. Earlier A, B, or C checks do not reset the D clock. Moving earlier checks cannot remove the first D’s unavoidable interval. The loaded first-D windows agree with those reconstructed from the opening due dates for all 24 aircraft. [S2, S5]

The six intervals coincide on day indices 2676 through 2681, corresponding to 20 through 25 May 2033. At least six aircraft must be down on those days, so availability can be no greater than 24 minus 6, which is 18. Consequently a worst-day availability of 19 is infeasible for this forecast and these rules. The baseline’s achieved value of 18 matches the upper bound. [S5, S15]
What the bound does and does not establish
Total downtime is 10248 aircraft days. Across 4481 daily observations, average maintenance load is 2.286990 aircraft. That is below the five-aircraft-down allowance implied by target 19. The failure comes from concentrated unavoidable occupancy rather than insufficient average capacity.
The bound is conditional on the fleet, check durations, early-start allowances, and fixed opening due dates. Reaching 19 would require changing the instance or one of those assumptions. It cannot be achieved by tuning this optimiser alone. The model does not impose hangar-bay, crew, or parts capacity limits, so those are not the cause of this bound. [S2, S15]
The D-core overlap, dates, aircraft names, workbook counts, and loaded schedule compliance were independently recalculated from the current local workbook during preparation of this report.

6 Operational constraints and measured outcomes
The operational prototype adds three hard rules in a separate JSON configuration. A busy period requires a minimum number of aircraft available within an inclusive date range. A displacement limit bounds the absolute movement of each check from its as-loaded start, including changes caused by rebuilding later checks. A dependency group requires a stated number of its named aircraft to remain available on every day. [S4, S16]
The rules are dummy scenarios for research. The canonical maintenance workbook remains the input. Both feasible-neighbour generation and greedy polish can apply the operational filter. A hotter temperature never overrides an operational breach. The loaded schedule is checked first and a scenario that it already breaks is rejected without a repair search. [S3, S4, S6]

9 Compact staggered and as loaded starts
This experiment addresses whether an initially staggered schedule differs from a compact overlapping schedule and the current as-loaded schedule. The implemented labels are bunched for compact, spread for staggered, and as-loaded for the current forecast. Each method runs from the same constructed start, without operational limits, so the measured start effect is not mixed with constraint effects. [S4, S6, S7]
Construction of the three starts
The opening check is pinned to its due date by the maintenance replay. The implemented variants therefore move check index 1, the next check on each aircraft. Bunched moves it toward the fleet median start, clamped inside its legal window. Spread selects the legal-window endpoint furthest from that median. Each aircraft’s later checks are then rebuilt. The compact case aims to cluster checks across aircraft; it never permits overlapping checks on the same aircraft. [S2, S4]

The variants preserve aircraft, check identities, durations, opening due dates, and rule compliance. Validation already represented in the project
The source includes tests for baseline maintenance replay, acceptance decisions, feasible neighbours, seeded behaviour, best-so-far protection, tabu polish, operational limits, start construction, and the numeric keep-or-drop rule. The priority tuning records explicitly mark all 74 schedules as rule compliant. The final study results separately record zero operational breaches for loose and medium. Recorded validity covers the modelled maintenance rules and dummy limits, rather than all requirements of a deployed fleet planning system. [S10, S19]
Remaining work before broader claims
For a complete comparison under operational limits, measure candidate runs on the loose and medium scenarios using the same neighbourhoods, start, runtime rule, and fixed seeds as priority polish. Keep the tight result labelled as an invalid loaded start unless a repair search or a separate feasibility analysis is performed.
For stronger evidence on initial stagger, define a quantitative measure of compactness or overlap before constructing more extreme starts. Repeat candidate runs across seeds if the aim is a robust stochastic comparison. If starting maintenance ages or opening due dates are changed, treat those as a different instance and recompute the forced-core bound.
For performance claims, reduce deadline overshoot and measure runs in a consistent environment. The deterministic tuned baseline has the same score but different recorded timings of 96.5, 115.6, and 63.3 seconds across study contexts. Thus the available timing ratio describes the decision records, not an isolated hardware-controlled speed benchmark.
Presentation corrections
Explain that the tested candidate can temporarily lower worst-day availability, uses fixed escape temperature, and returns best-so-far. Distinguish the proof that 19 is impossible from the rejection of the tight loaded start. Use the local JSON values instead of mixing old slide metrics or the regenerated cloud dataset. The source handover filename includes 30 October, but it is an existing report label; the current evidence snapshot is the local results available on 12 October 2026. [S1, S3, S7, S21]

11 Suggested presentation narrative
Iteration 1 foundation
“At the end of Iteration 1, priority-based selection gave us a practical method. It targeted the checks that affected our worst availability days, rebuilt each aircraft’s later checks when a move occurred, and updated the availability efficiently. On the local forecast it improved worst-day availability from 15 to 18 aircraft.”
What I added in Semester 2
“In Semester 2, I investigated whether allowing temporary worse moves could improve the result further. I retained the maintenance logic and the priority search. First I added classic simulated annealing acceptance. I then developed basin-hopping, where the search leaves a good schedule, walks through a different region, and uses priority polish to improve it again. The best schedule is protected throughout.”
What the experiments showed
“Classic simulated annealing did not improve the published baseline in the three recorded local runs. Basin-hopping did improve smoothness slightly, but the improvement became much smaller once I compared it fairly with a tuned priority method. The tuned priority result was 1.391860 standard deviation. The median basin-hopping result was 1.390417, about a 0.10 percent reduction. Two of the three runs timed out, and the median runtime exceeded five minutes. That does not meet the adoption rule.”
Why I stopped chasing availability 19
“I also established a bound on this forecast. A D check lasts 126 days but can only start up to 70 days early. That leaves 56 days when it must be in maintenance regardless of its legal start. Six aircraft share that unavoidable interval on 20 to 25 May 2033. With 24 aircraft, availability can only be 18 on those days. The baseline therefore reaches the best possible worst-day availability under these rules.”
Constraints and settings
“I added dummy busy periods, displacement limits, and dependency groups as hard rules. Loose and medium scenarios kept legal priority schedules at 18, although smoothing became harder. The tight pack was already invalid on the loaded plan, and this prototype does not repair it. In the 74-trial priority settings study, richer half-step neighbourhoods gave the strongest dependable gain, approximately 1.56 percent better smoothness than the original settings.”
Starting schedule and recommendation
“I compared compact, staggered, and as-loaded starts. The first check cannot move in this model, so these variants alter the next check and rebuild the later checks. All methods reached 18, but as-loaded gave the smoothest final result. This shows that the starting schedule matters, and simply spreading the next checks does not guarantee a better final plan. My recommendation is to retain priority-based selection with the tuned half-step neighbourhoods. The Semester 2 contribution is the measured comparison, the availability bound, the operational prototype, and the sensitivity evidence supporting that choice.”
This narrative describes the recorded experiments. Do not describe the candidate as cooling over time or the tight scenario as globally impossible without additional analysis.
