Priority Based Selection Hyperparameter Analysis
Fleet maintenance optimisation
David LongUniversity of Queensland and Northrop Grumman13 October 2026
Main finding
The recommended settings for the as-loaded forecast are half steps with first level selection and max_iters equal to 400. They achieve worst-day availability of 18 aircraft and smoothness of 1.391860, a 1.56 percent reduction in standard deviation from the default. Mean availability remains 21.713010 aircraft.
Worst-day availability of 18 is globally maximal under the first D check rules for this forecast. Six aircraft must be in D maintenance together on six dates in May 2033. Tuning therefore improves the distribution of availability around an already optimal worst day. The best smoothness found is an empirical result; the study does not establish a global smoothness optimum.

The analysis combines 74 historical runs with 34 fresh runs, covering 87 distinct parameter sets and four start schedules. It examines iteration budgets, shift magnitudes, ladder structure, ladder order, selection policy, interactions, local refinements, runtime variability, and sensitivity to legal changes in the start schedule.
Scope: savn_priority_based_selection.py on heavy_maintenance_forecast.xlsx, MxEvents. The simulated annealing and early D variants are outside this comparison.

Algorithm and hyperparameters
The baseline is a deterministic hill-climb using a sequence of neighbourhoods. For each neighbourhood, checks are ordered by the lowest availability during their active maintenance interval. The search scans priority levels from worst to best and stops at the first level containing an improving move. It then accepts the best move within that level and starts the next iteration at the first neighbourhood again.
A proposal moves one check by an offset and rebuilds the aircraft's later checks. Rebuilds enforce the maintenance sequence and recalculate due dates and legal start windows. Positive offsets may move a check later within its legal window; no check may start after its current due date. A larger improving shift can cross over schedules that are worse, but the algorithm never accepts a worsening endpoint.

What is and is not tunable
Only max_iters is exposed by savn_optimise. shift_sets is a local constant in that function, and first-priority-level selection is built into generate_neighbors. This study varies these algorithm settings in a separate driver. Temperature, cooling rate, tabu tenure, and a search random seed do not apply to this deterministic method.
Objective and fixed inputs
Scores are compared lexicographically as (minimum availability, negative standard deviation, mean availability). A gain of one aircraft on the worst day outranks any smoothness change. Standard deviation uses the population definition implemented by numpy.std. No weighted scalar objective is introduced.
Maintenance rules are fixed domain inputs: A 14 day duration 365 day interval 42 day early allowance; B 28 730 70; C 84 1460 70; D 126 2920 70. Changing these rules would change the scheduling problem rather than tune the optimiser.

Validation
The production default and study driver return the same complete schedule, score, and trace length. Every fresh endpoint passes complete maintenance-rule replay and a recomputation of daily availability. A separate boolean occupancy calculation verifies availability without the delta-array method. Every accepted score strictly improves, check counts and durations are conserved, and all 74 historical endpoints are marked rule-compliant. The nine existing baseline tests also pass in the analysis runtime.

A single plus-or-minus seven-day neighbourhood is the strongest converged single-magnitude setting in this sweep. It reaches 18 available aircraft but ends at standard deviation 1.470754, worse than the default and tuned ladders. A fourteen-day neighbourhood also reaches 18 quickly, after 188 accepted moves, but leaves standard deviation 1.474391.
The one-, two-, and three-day searches hit the 400-move cap at worst-day availability 17. These results show inefficient progress within the tested budget; they do not establish that the small-step neighbourhoods would converge at 17. The five-day run also hits its cap, even though it reaches 18.
Very wide single steps are poor on this workbook. The seventy-day setting accepts only two moves and converges at 15, while fifty-six days converges at 16. Wide jumps often lie outside legal windows or offer no improving endpoint. That explanation is consistent with the outcome but the historical study did not count rejected proposals, so it is not a measured rejection-rate result.
Implication for tuning
Retain multiple scales of movement. The strongest single neighbourhood does not replace a ladder, and larger offsets do not monotonically improve the result. Fine offsets are useful for later smoothness improvement when combined with earlier larger shifts.

The dense ladder of plus-or-minus 28, 21, 14, 7, 3, and 1 days is the best ordinary paired ladder in the historical sweep. Its standard deviation is 1.396502. A geometric ladder of 32, 16, 8, 4, and 2 days improves from 1.404470 to 1.399695 when a one-day final neighbourhood is added.
The coarse 70, 42, and 28 day ladder converges at worst-day availability 17. Two-level shortcuts reach 18 but lose smoothness. These observations support a mixture of coarse movement and fine finishing for this forecast. The exact ranking is path-dependent: the algorithm takes the first neighbourhood with an improvement, so adding or reordering a neighbourhood changes later schedules.
Half offsets within each neighbourhood
The previous tuned baseline uses four neighbourhoods: (-28, -14, 14, 28), (-14, -7, 7, 14), (-7, -3, 3, 7), and (-3, -1, 1, 3). The best improving move is selected among all offsets within the first improving priority level. This produces a different trajectory from trying each offset pair in a separate neighbourhood.
A single union of the original offsets returns standard deviation 1.413656. Adding 21- and one-day offsets to that union reaches 1.406534. Both are worse than the half-step ladder. This is measured evidence for retaining the layered search structure on this input, rather than a general guarantee that layering always wins.

First level returns all improving candidates in the first priority level that contains any, then takes the best. All levels takes the best improving candidate across every priority level for the active neighbourhood. First improving stops at the first improving candidate in priority level, start date, aircraft name, check index, and offset order. All three still require a strictly better lexicographic score.
The interaction table tests each policy on three different neighbourhood structures. This avoids assuming that a selection rule measured on the default transfers unchanged to the tuned ladder. Ties in candidate scores retain the first candidate in the deterministic iteration order.
The best endpoint in this interaction comparison uses half steps with first level selection and standard deviation 1.391860. The report recommends settings based on the complete extended comparison, including local refinements, rather than treating the selection comparison as a separate optimisation target.
Priority sorting directs proposals towards checks that cover low-availability days, but it is a search heuristic. Even all-level selection preserves worst-day availability through the score rule. Any smoothness differences arise from different accepted paths, not from one policy allowing worse worst days.
Fresh results use the same 800 safety cap. Historical default all-level selection ended at 1.415233; half-step all-level selection ended at 1.403198. Reproduced endpoints provide an additional consistency check.

Availability limits and operational scope
The mean is fixed by conserved total downtime. Over 4481 days, the 278 checks require 10248 aircraft days of maintenance, so mean availability is 24 minus 10248 divided by 4481, or 21.713010489. Changing only legal starts cannot improve the mean when all checks stay inside the comparison horizon. The third score component therefore does not discriminate among these runs.
Why worst day availability cannot exceed 18
Each aircraft's first D due date is its first A due date plus 2555 days. A, B, and C checks do not reset the D due clock. The first D duration is 126 days and its early allowance is 70 days. A D check with due day d can start only between d minus 70 and d. Every legal placement occupies the forced interval from d to d plus 56, with the end excluded.

These six forced intervals overlap on 20 to 25 May 2033. At least six aircraft must be unavailable on each of these dates, so at most 18 can be available. Since the tuned schedules achieve a worst day of 18 across the whole horizon, the primary objective is proven optimal for this forecast and the unchanged first D rules.

Reaching 19 would require changing a problem assumption, such as the D early allowance, first due clocks, or check duration, and must be treated as a separate scenario.
Operational constraints
The baseline validates maintenance rules, legal starts, non-overlap, and the horizon. It does not load operational_constraints.json and does not enforce busy periods, dependency groups, or displacement limits. A tuned schedule must be re-evaluated under any required operational constraints before it is used as an operational plan. Hyperparameter rankings under added constraints may differ.

Implementation and reproducibility
Applying the recommendation
The production module and source workbook remain unchanged. To apply the recommended neighbourhoods in savn_optimise, replace the local shift_sets list with the neighbourhoods listed on the local refinement page. Preserve maintenance rebuilds and lexicographic acceptance. If the recommended policy is first level, no generate_neighbors change is needed; alternative policies require the corresponding study selection behaviour to be made explicit.
Expose shift_sets as a function argument for repeatable comparison rather than editing a local constant for every experiment. Keep the frozen default as the default argument for baseline comparisons. Record the chosen cap, the accepted-move count, and whether the search stopped naturally. An 800 cap provides room for the observed start-schedule sensitivity experiments without requiring 800 moves.
Reproducing the evidence
Run .scratch/priority-hyperparameter-analysis/run_analysis.py with the Python runtime listed in the source record. The script resumes completed trials only when source workbook and production hashes match. To force a clean rerun, use a fresh study folder or preserve and rename the existing fresh-results.json before running again. The original study also skips existing names and must not be mistaken for a fresh execution when rerun against its stored file.
fresh-results.json contains complete endpoint metrics, daily distributions, hashes, and accepted-move trajectories. Each fresh run also has a schedule JSON; start_41.json, start_42.json, and start_43.json preserve the sensitivity inputs. analysis-summary.json records combined counts and the selected configuration. The historical run table and fresh catalogue follow in the appendices.
Limits of the recommendation
This study covers one 24-aircraft forecast and three small legal perturbations of it. It does not test an independent fleet, a different horizon, larger data, changed maintenance rules, or operational constraints. The search is deterministic and the setting sweep is deliberately selected rather than exhaustive. The measured best is a tuned baseline for this problem, and future method comparisons should report comparable outcomes and budgets against it.
The start-variant medians describe sensitivity across three fixed inputs. Timing medians describe three repeated executions of one deterministic input. Neither supports statistical significance claims. Report a cap-limited endpoint as incomplete convergence and avoid selecting settings solely from smoothness when their worst-day availability is lower.
